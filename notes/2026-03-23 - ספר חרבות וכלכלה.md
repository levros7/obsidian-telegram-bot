Here's a deeper dive on **"חרבות הכלכלה"** by Eyal Hashkes:

---

**The two-book framework:**

The two books together present the two sides of geo-economics: "Swords of the Economy" describes how the economy serves as a powerful weapon, while "Bridges of the Economy" describes how the economy can drive improved diplomatic relations.

**What the book covers in detail:**

You'll learn how misuse of economic levers led to Hamas's October 7th surprise attack, how Israel defended against the Arab boycott that threatened to destroy it, and how the West together with Israel launched an economic offensive against Iran and terror organizations. It also examines what distinguishes Trump's approach to economic punishment from his predecessors, who's winning the US-China superpower competition, and why the broadest sanctions regime in history failed to stop the Russia-Ukraine war.

**Hashkes on Israel's post-war economy (from a recent Maariv interview):**

- The war hurt Israel's economic momentum but didn't crush it. Israel experienced an extreme geopolitical scenario and the economy stayed on its feet.
- Debt-to-GDP ratio went from ~60% before the war to over 70%, which is natural during wartime.
- Opportunities ahead include return of human capital, increased hi-tech investment, and growth in defense industries. Risks include brain drain, an inflated defense budget, and regional instability.
- He notes that because the war was the focus, growth-accelerating reforms didn't advance.

**The author's approach** (from a review of his first book): Rather than grand peace plans, Hashkes proposes a series of independent steps that can each improve the situation incrementally — a conservative, pragmatic approach of gradual progress.
