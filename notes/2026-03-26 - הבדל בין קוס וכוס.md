# הבדל בין קוס וכוס
**Date:** 2026-03-26

---

**🧑 You:** קוס או כוס

**🤖 Claude:** **Correction:** "קוס" → **"כוס"** (with כ, not ק).

**Why it matters:** "כוס" (kos) means "cup/glass." Spelling it with ק changes the word to a vulgar slang term. In professional writing, this mix-up could be embarrassing — always use כ.

**🧑 You:** קללה או כללה

**🤖 Claude:** Both are real words, but they mean different things:

- **קללה** (klalá) = **curse** (ק is correct here)
- **כללה** (klalá) = **she included** (from the root כ.ל.ל)

**Why it matters:** These are two separate words, not a spelling mistake. The first letter changes the meaning entirely. Context tells you which one to use:

- "הוא קיבל **קללה**" = He received a curse
- "היא **כללה** את כולם" = She included everyone

Which one did you mean to use?

**🧑 You:** ברח או ברך

**🤖 Claude:** Both are real words with different meanings:

- **ברח** (barákh) = **he escaped/fled** (from the root ב.ר.ח)
- **ברך** (beréch) = **knee**, or **ברך** (berákh) = **he blessed** (from the root ב.ר.כ)

**Why it matters:** ח and כ sound similar but swapping them completely changes the meaning. Context is key:

- "הוא **ברח** מהבית" = He fled from the house
- "הוא **ברך** אותי" = He blessed me
- "כאב לי ה**ברך**" = My knee hurts
