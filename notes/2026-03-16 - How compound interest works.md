The core idea is simple: **you earn interest on your interest**, not just on what you deposited.

The formula is `A = P × (1 + r)ⁿ` — where P is your starting amount, r is the annual rate, and n is years.

What makes it powerful is the exponent. In the early years, the green bar (interest) barely grows. But by year 20–30, it towers over your original deposit. That acceleration is the "compound effect" — each year's interest becomes part of the base for next year's calculation.

A few things to play with in the widget:
- Drag **Years** to 30–40 and watch the interest explode
- Try **rate 3% vs 10%** — the difference is dramatic over time
- Notice that doubling the rate more than doubles your final balance (nonlinear!)

A rough rule of thumb: **the Rule of 72** — divide 72 by your annual rate to get roughly how many years it takes to double your money. At 7%, that's about 10 years.
